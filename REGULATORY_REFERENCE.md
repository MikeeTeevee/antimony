## Title II - Auditor Independence

### SEC. 201 - Services Outside the Scope of Practice of Auditors {#sec-201}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 78j–1

#### Prohibited Non-Audit Services

Except as provided in subsection (b), it shall be unlawful for a registered public accounting firm (and any associated person of that firm) that provides audit services to an issuer to provide to that issuer, contemporaneously with the audit services, any non-audit service, including—

(1) bookkeeping or other accounting services related to the accounting records or financial statements of the audit client;

(2) financial information systems design and implementation;

(3) appraisal or valuation services, fairness opinions, or contribution-in-kind reports;

(4) actuarial services;

(5) internal audit outsourcing services;

(6) management functions or human resources;

(7) broker or dealer, investment adviser, or investment banking services;

(8) legal services and expert services unrelated to the audit; and

(9) any other service that the Board determines, by regulation, is impermissible.

#### Permitted Non-Audit Services

The prohibition in subsection (a) shall not apply to the provision of a non-audit service, if the service is approved in advance by the audit committee of the issuer, in accordance with section 202.

**Compliance Areas:**
- Access Controls over audit engagement scope
- Documentation of service authorizations
- Change Management for audit scope modifications
- Testing of scope compliance

---

### SEC. 202 - Preapproval Requirements {#sec-202}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 78j–1(b)

#### Preapproval by Audit Committee

Any non-audit service provided to an issuer by the same registered public accounting firm that provides audit services to that issuer shall be approved in advance by the audit committee of the issuer.

#### Audit Committee Authority

The audit committee shall—

(1) establish policies and procedures for the preapproval of non-audit services provided by the auditor, subject to the requirement that all such services shall be preapproved by the audit committee;

(2) preapprove all permitted non-audit services, either an explicit case-by-case basis before the initiation of each service, or as part of the audit committee's approval of the scope of the audit services;

(3) consider whether services preapproved might impair the independence of the auditor; and

(4) require the registered public accounting firm to report to the audit committee, at least annually, regarding all non-audit services so provided.

**Compliance Areas:**
- Formal audit committee processes and documentation
- Preapproval procedures and authorization workflows
- Regular reporting mechanisms
- Independence assessment procedures

---

### SEC. 203 - Audit Partner Rotation {#sec-203}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 78j–1(c)

#### Rotation Requirement

The Commission shall establish rules, in accordance with this section, such that each registered public accounting firm shall rotate its lead audit partner (and the concurring partner) off the particular audit engagement for the same issuer after 5 consecutive years, and such auditor may not resume a lead audit partner role on the audit until at least 5 years have passed.

#### Audit Partner Rotation

(1) Each registered public accounting firm shall adopt, maintain, and enforce policies and procedures reasonably designed to implement the requirements of subsection (a).

(2) Notwithstanding subsection (a), in the case of an audit engagement for an issuer that is an accelerated filer (as that term is defined in section 12b–2 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 (17 CFR 240.12b–2)), the Commission may, by rule or regulation, require that each registered public accounting firm rotate its lead audit partner off the particular audit engagement for the same issuer after every 5 years.

**Compliance Areas:**
- Change Management - audit partner assignment tracking
- Documentation of audit partner rotations
- Formal procedures and authorization controls
- Periodic review and testing procedures

---

### SEC. 204 - Auditor Reports to Audit Committees {#sec-204}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 78j–1(d)

#### Required Auditor Communications

Each registered public accounting firm that performs an audit shall, at least once annually, prepare and furnish to the audit committee of the issuer a formal written report describing—

(1) all critical accounting policies and practices to be used;

(2) all alternative treatments of financial information within generally accepted accounting principles that have been discussed with management of the issuer, ramifications of the use of such alternative disclosures and treatments, and the treatment preferred by the registered public accounting firm; and

(3) other material written communications between the registered public accounting firm and the management of the issuer, such as any management letter or schedule of unadjusted differences.

**Compliance Areas:**
- Documentation of auditor communications
- Formal written reporting procedures
- Audit committee engagement and documentation
- Alternative accounting treatment analysis and documentation

### SEC. 206 - Conflicts of Interest {#sec-206}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 78j–1(f)

#### Auditor Independence Restrictions

(a) RESTRICTION ON EMPLOYMENT BY AUDIT CLIENT.—It shall be unlawful for a registered public accounting firm to provide audit services to an issuer if, at any point during the one-year period preceding the initiation of the audit services, the firm employed in an accounting or financial management function any person who participated in any capacity in the provision of internal audit services to that issuer.

(b) INTERPRETATION.—For purposes of this section, auditor independence requirements shall be interpreted consistently with the statement entitled "Independence: Interpretation of Rule 101 of the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct."

#### Documentation and Testing
- Formal processes for identifying conflicts of interest
- Background checks and historical employment verification
- Documentation of independence confirmations
- Periodic testing of compliance

## Title III - Corporate Responsibility

### SEC. 301 - Public Company Audit Committees {#sec-301}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 78j–1(m)

#### Establishment of Rules

The Commission shall, by rule, direct the national securities exchanges and national securities associations to prohibit the listing of any security of an issuer unless the issuer has complied with the requirements of this section.

#### Audit Committee Authority

The audit committee shall—

(1) be directly responsible for the appointment, compensation, and oversight of the work of any registered public accounting firm employed by that issuer (including resolution of disagreements between management and the auditor regarding financial reporting) for the purpose of preparing or issuing an audit report or related work;

(2) establish procedures for—
   (A) the receipt, retention, and treatment of complaints received by the issuer regarding accounting, internal accounting controls, or auditing matters; and
   (B) the confidential, anonymous submission by employees of the issuer of concerns regarding questionable accounting or auditing matters;

(3) have the authority to engage independent counsel and other advisers, as it determines necessary to carry out its duties.

**Compliance Areas:**
- Formal audit committee charter and policies
- Documented procedures for auditor oversight
- Whistleblower procedures and documentation
- Advisor engagement and management
- Internal controls over audit matters

### SEC. 302 - Corporate Responsibility for Financial Reports {#sec-302}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 7241

#### Certification of Periodic Reports

(a) REQUIREMENT OF CERTIFICATION.—The Commission shall require, for each company filing periodic reports under section 13(a) or 15(d) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 (15 U.S.C. 78m, 78o(d)), that the principal executive officer or officers and the principal financial officer or officers, or persons performing similar functions, certify in each annual or quarterly report filed or submitted under either such section—

(1) that the signing officer has reviewed the report;

(2) that based on the officer's knowledge, the report does not contain any untrue statement of a material fact or omit to state a material fact necessary in order to make the statements made, in light of the circumstances under which such statements were made, not misleading;

(3) that based on such officer's knowledge, the financial statements, and other financial information included in the report, fairly present in all material respects the financial condition and results of operations of the issuer as of, and for, the periods presented in the report;

(4) that the signing officers—
   (A) are responsible for establishing and maintaining internal controls;
   (B) have designed such internal controls to ensure that material information relating to the issuer and its consolidated subsidiaries is made known to such officers;
   (C) have evaluated the effectiveness of such internal controls within the previous 90 days; and
   (D) have presented in the report their conclusions about the effectiveness of such internal controls based on their evaluation;

(5) that the signing officers have disclosed to the auditors and the audit committee—
   (A) all significant deficiencies in the design or operation of internal controls;
   (B) any fraud, whether or not material, that involves management or other employees who have a significant role in the issuer's internal controls;

(6) a list of any material changes in internal controls or in other factors that could significantly affect internal controls;

(7) that the signing officers have indicated in the report whether there were significant changes in internal controls or other factors during the most recent fiscal quarter that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, the issuer's internal controls over financial reporting.

#### Fraud Liability

(b) FAILURE TO CERTIFY.—If an officer of an issuer fails to certify any periodic report required by subsection (a), and such officer knows or should know that such report does not comport with all the certifications required by subsection (a), such officer shall forfeit any bonus or other incentive-based or equity-based compensation received during the 12-month period following the first public issuance or filing of the document incorporating such false certification, and shall be jointly and severally liable with other such officers for any restatement of the issuer's financial statements that is required as a result of noncompliance by such officer with certification requirements.

**Compliance Areas:**
- Internal control documentation and assessment
- Officer certification procedures
- Material weakness and deficiency identification
- Disclosure procedures and controls
- Fraud identification and reporting
- Compensation clawback mechanisms

### SEC. 303 - Improper Influence on Conduct of Audits {#sec-303}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 78dd–1

#### Prohibition on Improper Influence

(a) IN GENERAL.—It shall be unlawful, in violation of the antifraud provisions of the securities laws, for any officer or director of an issuer, or any other person acting under the direction thereof, to take any action to fraudulently influence, coerce, manipulate, or mislead any independent public or registered public accounting firm engaged in the performance of an audit of the financial statements of that issuer for the purpose of rendering such financial statements materially misleading.

#### Documented Procedures

Organizations must maintain documented procedures to prevent and detect improper influence on audit activities.

**Compliance Areas:**
- Documented anti-coercion policies
- Audit independence safeguards
- Segregation of duties between management and auditors
- Regular training and acknowledgment procedures
- Investigation and remediation procedures

---

### SEC. 304 - Forfeiture of Certain Bonuses and Profits {#sec-304}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 7243

#### Requirement for Restatement

Whenever an issuer is required to prepare an accounting restatement due to the material noncompliance of that issuer, as a result of misconduct, with any financial reporting requirement under the securities laws, the chief executive officer and chief financial officer of that issuer shall reimburse the issuer for—

(1) any bonus or other incentive-based or equity-based compensation received during the 12-month period following the first public issuance or filing of the document incorporating the financial statements; and

(2) any profits realized from the sale of securities of that issuer during that 12-month period.

**Compliance Areas:**
- Compensation tracking and documentation systems
- Restatement procedures and controls
- Clawback authorization and execution
- Securities transaction monitoring
- Audit trail of compensation adjustments

### SEC. 306 - Insider Trades During Pension Fund Blackout Periods {#sec-306}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 7244

#### Prohibition on Insider Trading During Blackout Period

(a) PROHIBITION ON INSIDER TRADING DURING BLACKOUT PERIODS.—Except to the extent otherwise provided by rule of the Commission, it shall be unlawful for any officer or director of an issuer of any equity security (other than an exempted security), or any other person who is subject to section 16 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 (15 U.S.C. 78p) with respect to such issuer, to purchase, sell, or otherwise acquire or transfer any equity security of that issuer during any blackout period with respect to such equity security.

#### Blackout Period Definition

The term "blackout period," with respect to the equity securities of any issuer—

(1) means any period of more than 3 consecutive business days during which the ability of not fewer than 50 percent of the participants or beneficiaries under all individual account plans (as defined in section 3(34) of the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974) maintained by the issuer to purchase, sell, or otherwise acquire or transfer an interest in any equity of that issuer held in such an individual account plan is temporarily suspended by the issuer or a fiduciary of the plan.

**Compliance Areas:**
- Blackout period identification and tracking
- Insider notification procedures
- Trading restriction enforcement
- Documentation and audit trail
- Exception and exemption management

## Title IV - Enhanced Financial Disclosures

### SEC. 401 - Disclosures in Periodic Reports {#sec-401}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 78m

#### Requirement for Disclosure of Off-Balance Sheet Items

(a) DISCLOSURES REQUIRED.—The Commission shall issue rules providing that each annual report filed with the Commission shall contain selected financial data relating to material changes in the balance sheet, income statement, and cash flow statement of the issuer.

#### Material Off-Balance Sheet Transactions

The Commission shall require disclosure of all material off-balance sheet transactions, arrangements, obligations (including contingent obligations), and other relationships of the issuer that may have a material current or future effect on financial condition, changes in financial condition, results of operations, liquidity, capital expenditures, capital resources, or significant components of revenues or expenses.

#### Fraud Liability

If any document filed with the Commission contains a certification that does not comply with the requirements of this section, each officer or director of the issuer who signed such certification shall have personal liability for any restatement of the issuer's financial statements.

**Compliance Areas:**
- Off-balance sheet transaction identification and documentation
- Financial data integrity controls
- Completeness testing procedures
- Disclosure authorization procedures
- Regular management certification

### SEC. 402 - Enhanced Conflict of Interest Provisions {#sec-402}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 78m

#### Prohibition on Personal Loans to Executives

(a) PROHIBITION.—It shall be unlawful for any issuer (as defined in section 2 of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002), directly or indirectly, including through any subsidiary, to extend or maintain credit, to arrange for the extension of credit, or to renew an extension of credit, in the form of a personal loan to or for any director or executive officer (or equivalent thereof) of that issuer.

#### Exceptions

This section does not prohibit any home improvement and manufactured home loans, extension of credit under a charge card, extension of credit under an open end credit plan, or extension of credit by a broker or dealer registered under section 15 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, if the extension of credit is made in the ordinary course of the business of the broker or dealer.

**Compliance Areas:**
- Loan authorization and policy documentation
- Conflict of interest identification procedures
- Personal transaction monitoring
- Exception documentation and approval
- Regular compliance testing

### SEC. 404 - Management Assessment of Internal Controls {#sec-404}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 7262

#### (a) RULES REQUIRED

The Commission shall prescribe rules requiring each annual report required by section 13(a) or 15(d) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 (15 U.S.C. 78m or 78o(d)) to contain an internal control report, which shall—

(1) state the responsibility of management for establishing and maintaining an adequate internal control structure and procedures for financial reporting; and

(2) contain an assessment, as of the end of the most recent fiscal year of the issuer, of the effectiveness of the internal control structure and procedures of the issuer for financial reporting.

#### (b) INTERNAL CONTROL EVALUATION AND REPORTING

With respect to the internal control assessment required by subsection (a), each registered public accounting firm that prepares or issues the audit report for the issuer shall attest to, and report on, the assessment made by the management of the issuer. An attestation made under this subsection shall be made in accordance with standards for attestation engagements issued or adopted by the Board. Any such attestation shall not be the subject of a separate engagement.

#### (c) ATTESTATION REQUIREMENT

The standards referred to in subsection (b) shall require the attestation engagement to be performed in accordance with generally accepted auditing standards, as may be modified or supplemented by the Board, and shall require the auditor to attest to, and report on—

(1) whether the auditor obtained adequate evidence to support management's assessment of the effectiveness of the issuer's internal control structure and procedures for financial reporting; and

(2) the auditor's assessment, based upon his or her examination, of the effectiveness of the internal control structure and procedures of the issuer for financial reporting.

#### (d) ASSESSMENT OF MANAGEMENT'S ASSESSMENT

(1) IN GENERAL.—Rules issued by the Commission pursuant to subsection (a) shall require management to assess the effectiveness of internal controls as of a date within 120 days prior to the filing of the annual report.

(2) LIMITATION ON SCOPE.—The scope of the internal control assessment required by paragraph (1) shall not include an assessment of the overall effectiveness of the internal control structure and procedures.

#### (e) AUDITOR JUDGMENT OF DISCLOSURE

In prescribing rules to implement the requirements of subsection (a), the Commission may include requirements for disclosure about the effectiveness of the internal control structure and procedures of the issuer for financial reporting.

#### Key Control Areas Under SEC. 404

**Access Controls:**
- User access to financial systems and data
- Segregation of duties in financial processes
- User authentication and authorization

**Change Management:**
- Control of changes to financial systems
- Approval and testing of system changes
- Documentation of change procedures

**Financial Reporting:**
- Completeness and accuracy of financial data
- Timeliness of financial close and reporting
- Proper recording of transactions

**IT Controls:**
- System security and monitoring
- Data backup and recovery procedures
- System availability and integrity

**Documentation:**
- Complete documentation of all controls
- Evidence of control execution
- Testing and evaluation results

### SEC. 405 - Exemption {#sec-405}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 7262

#### Exemption for Smaller Reporting Companies

(a) DEFINITION.—For purposes of this section, the term "smaller reporting company" has the meaning given that term by the Commission.

(b) EXEMPTION.—The Commission shall exempt smaller reporting companies from the requirements of section 404(b), relating to the auditor's attestation on the assessment by management of the effectiveness of internal controls.

#### Application

The exemption provided under subsection (b) shall not apply to smaller reporting companies that—

(1) are investment companies; or

(2) are subject to regulations of the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board.

### SEC. 406 - Code of Ethics for Senior Financial Officers {#sec-406}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 7264

#### Requirement to Adopt Code

The Commission shall require, for each issuer having a class of securities registered under section 12 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, that the issuer disclose whether or not, and if not, the reason therefor, such issuer has adopted a code of ethics for senior financial officers, applicable to its principal financial officer and controller (or persons performing similar functions).

#### Contents of Code

The code of ethics for senior financial officers shall include standards that reasonably promote—

(1) honest and ethical conduct, including the ethical handling of actual or apparent conflicts of interest between personal and professional relationships;

(2) full, accurate, and understandable disclosure in reports and documents that a company submits to the Commission and in other public communications containing financial information;

(3) compliance with applicable governmental laws, rules, and regulations;

(4) the prompt internal reporting of code violations; and

(5) accountability for adherence to the code.

#### Public Disclosure

Any code of ethics adopted in accordance with the requirements of this subsection shall be filed with the Commission.

**Compliance Areas:**
- Code of ethics development and approval
- Documentation of code requirements
- Officer acknowledgment and training
- Disclosure and public filing procedures
- Annual compliance certification

### SEC. 407 - Disclosure of Audit Committee Financial Expert {#sec-407}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 7265

#### Disclosure Requirement

The Commission shall issue rules requiring each issuer, together with periodic reports filed under section 13(a) or section 15(d) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, to disclose whether or not, and if not, the reason therefor, the audit committee of that issuer has at least one member who is a financial expert, as such term is defined by the Commission.

#### Financial Expert Definition

The Commission shall define the term "financial expert" to mean a person who has, through education and experience as a public accountant or auditor or a principal financial officer, controller, or accounting officer of a publicly traded company, or from other relevant experience—

(1) an understanding of generally accepted accounting principles and financial statements;

(2) the ability to assess the general application of such principles in connection with the accounting for estimates, accruals, and reserves;

(3) experience analyzing and evaluating financial statements of generally comparable breadth and complexity to those presented by the issuer;

(4) an understanding of internal controls over financial reporting; and

(5) an understanding of audit committee functions.

**Compliance Areas:**
- Audit committee member qualification assessment
- Financial expert identification and documentation
- Disclosure procedures and accuracy
- Ongoing qualification maintenance
- Training and development tracking

### SEC. 408 - Enhanced Review of Periodic Disclosures by Issuers {#sec-408}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 7266

#### Enhanced Review

The Commission shall adopt rules requiring that each issuer of securities registered under section 12 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 shall review and update on a periodic basis (not less frequently than once every 90 days) the disclosure controls and procedures (including those for management's certifications) of that issuer, and shall make certifications with respect to those disclosure controls and procedures in the manner and within the timeframe that the Commission, by rule, shall specify.

#### Disclosure Controls and Procedures

For purposes of this section, the term "disclosure controls and procedures" means controls and procedures of an issuer that are designed to ensure that information required to be disclosed by the issuer in the reports that it files or submits under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 is recorded, processed, summarized, and reported within the time periods specified in the Commission's rules and forms.

**Compliance Areas:**
- Disclosure control design and documentation
- Periodic review procedures (minimum quarterly)
- Effectiveness testing and evaluation
- Management certification procedures
- Documentation and audit trails

---

### SEC. 409 - Real Time Issuer Disclosures {#sec-409}

**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 7266a

#### Real Time Disclosures

The Commission shall adopt rules requiring that each issuer of securities registered under section 12 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 disclose to the public on a rapid and current basis such additional information concerning material changes in the financial condition or operations of the issuer, in plain English, which in the Commission's judgment will enhance investor protection and promote efficiency in the market.

#### Technical Requirements

Such rules shall require that a registrant disclose information under this subsection—

(1) in such manner as the Commission determines to be appropriate (which may include by Internet or other electronic means); and

(2) at such times as the Commission determines to be appropriate (which shall be at least once every business day, unless the Commission determines that such disclosure would impose undue burden).

**Compliance Areas:**
- Material change identification procedures
- Rapid disclosure mechanisms
- System monitoring for material events
- Documentation of disclosure timing
- Internal controls over disclosure process
